Counted as upside, not in the numbers

  • Every other country where English is official but not the home language: India, Nigeria, the Philippines, South Africa, Singapore and the rest. Apple's own list shows English (U.K.) as the default store language in most of them. Frank's ruling, 8 Sep.
  • Undiagnosed and wrongly diagnosed people. The counts above are of identified people. No source gives a multiplier, so none is applied. Frank's ruling, 8 Sep.
  • Conditions with no per-country count in hand: ALS, Parkinson's with speech loss, dementia with speech loss, cerebral palsy and Down syndrome in adults, traumatic brain injury, laryngectomy. WHO gives worldwide totals for some (Parkinson's over 8.5 million, dementia 57 million); the share who cannot rely on speech has no source yet.
  • Outpatient speech therapy practices, ALS clinics, and rehab centers outside the US. The US labor statistics page blocked the request and ASHA's site answers with a bot challenge; other countries not yet read.
  • Special education outside the US and England. Not yet read.
  • Next languages. The same method points at the largest non-English store markets once English shows traction; no revenue counted until Frank decides.

All three models combined

Consumer: one-time app buyers and the Family Tether

School districts: EZvoxa Pro seats on purchase orders

Clinical: EZvoxa Pro seats for speech therapy, stroke and other rehab, ALS, any loss-of-speech center

Conversion inputs for the selected level (Frank's to set)

Buyer share: new one-time buyers each year as a share of the counted consumer market. Subscription attach: the share of all buyers to date who hold the Family Tether at year end. School seat share: new seats each year as a share of the counted school market. Clinic facility share: the share of facilities that have bought by that year, times seats per facility.
