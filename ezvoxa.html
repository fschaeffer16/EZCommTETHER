01 — THE FIRST VOICE

The old device did something important. It taught him that words exist.

But as Evan got older, too many everyday sentences demanded too many screens. A simple thought could cost a minute of menus. Eventually the friction became the message.

02 — THE TURN

When a board was wrong, Evan ignored it. When it was right, he used it without being asked.

That changed the job. Instead of asking him to adapt to the software, the software had to adapt to him: visual, fast, familiar, and built around the life he was already living.

03 — THE PROOF

Then he opened the app himself and said what he wanted to watch. Unprompted.

That moment became the standard for every screen that followed: does it make communication easier enough that the user wants to reach for it?

Step 1 of 6

Start with what matters most.

Built around real life

A talker they can actually live with.

Huge picture buttons. Familiar people. Fast phrases. A full sentence builder when they need more. The visual cue comes first; the menu does not.

🖼️

Pictures are the buttons

Home boards, food, feelings, places, morning, night, friends and more use recognizable visual cues instead of walls of tiny words.

🩹

Hurt has its own door

A body map lets someone show where something hurts without building an entire sentence first. Communication, not medical advice.

👥

Their people

Custom photos and familiar faces make family, friends and school staff easier to recognize quickly.

🧩

Sentence Build + spelling

Phrase-first is not phrase-only. Users can expand into sentence building and a spelling board when they want to say something new.

💾

Backup & Restore

One file saves the setup so a caregiver can move it to a new phone without rebuilding the entire board from scratch.

What comes with it

Communication first. Extras second.

No made-up launch price. No made-up store date. The distinction is simple: the core talker is intended to be free; connected family features are the future premium layer.
