He didn’t need a better device. He needed one that would grow with him.

I had to find a way to meet him where he was. So I became the student and he became my teacher.

My name is Frank. My son Evan was born in August 2010. He is nonverbal. I am not a speech pathologist. I am a father who spent sixteen years learning every look on his face.

A four-thousand-dollar board taught him that words exist. Then he grew up, and a sentence like I’m tired, can I go to bed cost a minute of menus. He stopped paying. He wasn’t failing the device. The device was pricing him out of his own voice.

I didn’t design what came next. He walked me to it. When a board was wrong, he ignored it. When it was right, he used it without being asked. Then he took my phone, opened the app himself, and told me what he wanted to watch. Unprompted. Sixteen years. That had never happened.

EZvoxa is that talker. Phrase-first, on the phone in his pocket. His photos. His people. Hurt, so nobody has to guess. School, with the faces in the building. Family — I love you, I miss you, a birthday text. Family Alert is two taps to the people we choose. It is not 911. It is not a medical device. It is him, being heard.

It is not for sale yet. If you want the day it is, leave your email. If you want to see what he taught me, watch. If you want the whole story, it’s already written.
