# AAC Display/Interface Design for Young Children (3–12, nonverbal/minimally verbal): Evidence Review

Compiled 2026-09-09. Every bullet: authors, year, N, population, finding, URL. Population flags: **[TD]** = typically developing (no disability — findings are about learnability/perception, not proof of benefit for AAC users); **[DIS]** = participants with disabilities / complex communication needs (CCN).

---

## 1. Visual Scene Displays (VSDs) vs. grid displays

- **Drager, Light, Speltz, Fallon & Jeffries (2003)**, *JSLHR* 46:298–312. **N=30 [TD]** children aged 2;5–2;11, randomized 10 per condition: (a) grid/taxonomic, (b) grid/schematic, (c) integrated scene (VSD)/schematic; asked to locate 12 vocabulary items in a birthday-party play context over 4 learning sessions + 1 generalization session. **Children performed poorly in ALL conditions** but located significantly more items in the schematic scene condition. Authors concluded then-current dynamic display designs were "inappropriate for very young children." https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14700373/
- **Drager, Light, Carlson, D'Silva, Larsson, Pitkin & Stopper (2004)**, *JSLHR* 47:1133–1148. **N=30 [TD]** 3-year-olds (10 per condition), differing layouts and *menu-page approaches*. All conditions hard at first exposure; **after the first learning session children performed significantly better with contextual scene (VSD) format than grid format**; limited generalization to new vocabulary. Also (per secondary reports incl. Light et al. 2019 review): 3-year-olds navigated more accurately using **thumbnail miniatures of VSDs as menu items** than isolated symbols. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15603467/ ; https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/1092-4388(2004/084)
- **Light, Drager & Wilkinson (2012 infant eye-tracking, cited in Light, Wilkinson et al. 2019 review)** [TD]: infants 9–12 months in a split-screen paradigm **looked first and longest at photo VSDs vs. 4-symbol line-drawing grids**. Attention evidence only, not communication outcome. Reported in: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6436972/
- **Patenaude, McNaughton & Liang (2024/2025)**, *Journal of Special Education Technology*, "Using Visual Scene Displays With Young Children: An Evidence-Based Practice Synthesis." **14 studies; 12 met all CEC quality indicators; N=42 total [DIS]**, ages 3–8 (ASD n=34, Down syndrome n=2, IDD n=9). **37/42 participants positive effects, 0 negative**; outcomes: symbolic communicative turns (6 studies), requesting (3), sight-word reading (2), comments (1). Concludes **VSDs/video VSDs meet CEC criteria as an evidence-based practice** for young children with ASD/IDD. Caveats: all single-case designs, mostly researcher-implemented, heavy Penn State/related lab overlap. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/01626434241263061 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12645487/
- **Gevarter, O'Reilly et al. (2014)**, *JADD* 44:2464–2474. **N=3 [DIS]** preschool boys with ASD, multielement design comparing iPad displays: Widgit symbol button (grid-style), **photographic hotspot in a scene**, and combined. **2 of 3 children acquired mands faster/more consistently with the photo hotspot** than symbol button; third mastered all. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-014-2115-9
- **Therrien & Light (2016)**, *AAC* 32:163–174. **N=2 [DIS]** preschoolers with CCN (+6 TD peers): iPad VSD app + turn-taking intervention increased communicative turns with peers (P1 large sustained gains of 30–46 turns/10 min; P2 gains not maintained). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27414808/
- **Drager, Light, Currall et al. (2019)**, *J Intellect Dev Disabil* 44(3):321–336. **N=9 [DIS]** students aged 8–20 with severe disability; VSDs + just-in-time programming; multiple probe across participants — **all 9 increased symbolic communication turns**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31263379/
- **Hot spots / dynamic VSD arrangement**: Olin, Reichle et al. (2010), *AJSLP* "Examining Dynamic Visual Scene Displays: Implications for Arranging and Teaching Symbol Selection" — implications for hotspot arrangement/teaching. https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/1058-0360(2010/09-0001)

**What the evidence actually shows:** For beginning communicators / earliest language stages, VSDs beat grids for *learnability* (TD toddler experiments) and have single-case-design evidence of increasing *communication turns and requests* in disabled 3–8-year-olds (EBP status per CEC standards). There is **no evidence that VSDs are better for children who are past the beginning-communicator stage**; grids support requesting and broader vocabulary growth, and the field's consensus (Light et al. 2019 reviews) is VSDs for entry, transitioning to grids/hybrids as language grows — but that transition itself is essentially untested.

## 2. Symbol iconicity and personalization

- **Mirenda & Locke (1989)**, *JSHD* 54:131–140. **N=40 [DIS]** nonspeaking individuals with intellectual disabilities (some with autism/physical impairments). Transparency hierarchy: **real objects > color photos > B&W photos > line drawings (PCS, Picsyms, Rebus…) > Blissymbols/written words**. Foundational evidence that photos are easier than line drawings for this population. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2709831/
- **The "3–12% recognition" studies — identified:**
  - **Haupt & Alant (2002)**, *S Afr J Commun Disord* 49:40–49. **N: rural Zulu 10-year-olds [TD]** matching 36 spoken labels to PCS on a commercial overlay: only **2.8% (strict) to 11.1% (lenient) of symbols were iconic**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14968701/
  - **Basson & Alant (2005)**, *S Afr J Commun Disord* 52:4–12. **[TD]** urban 6-year-old Afrikaans speakers, 16-symbol PCS overlay: **iconicity 12.5%** (symbols with >50% accuracy); training improved recognition. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17902397/
  - Related: **Lynch (2000)**, *Child Lang Teach Ther* — 42% of sampled PCS not easily guessable by young TD children. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/ACS-2000-10106
  - Caveat: both Alant-lab samples are non-US cultural/linguistic contexts; the numbers generalize as "PCS line drawings are largely NOT guessable by young children," not as precise universal rates.
- **Worah, McNaughton, Light & Benedek-Wood (2015)**, *Int J Speech-Lang Pathol* 17(5):460–469 (the "2015 Penn State study"). **N=40 [TD]** children 2.5–3.5 yrs, post-test-only between-subjects RCT: **Developmentally Appropriate Symbols (DAS)** — derived from how young children conceptualize/draw early vocabulary (whole people, objects and activities in context, color/size emphasis) — vs. **PCS**. **DAS group correctly identified significantly more symbols**; no preference difference. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25592055/ ; https://aac.psu.edu/2015/04/23/representing-aac-vocabulary-for-young-children-publication/
- **Light, McNaughton & Caron (2019)**, *AAC* 35(1):26–41, "New and emerging AAC technology supports…state of the science." Argues for **personalized VSDs using photos of the child's own familiar people/events** as developmentally appropriate first representations (language is learned in social interaction; personalized photos capture those events); reviews VSD/JIT/T2L evidence base. Review, not primary data. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30648902/ ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07434618.2018.1557251
- **Wilkinson & Light (2014)**, *AAC* 30:130–146. **N=18 [DIS+TD]** school-aged (TD, ASD, Down syndrome, other ID) eye tracking on photos: **human figures captured fixation within ~1.5 s in all groups**, ~36–40% of viewing time — supports including people in VSD photos. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24773517/

**Bottom line:** Standard symbol-set line drawings are poorly recognized by children under ~6 without teaching (STRONG for the fact; children do learn them with instruction). Child-derived/contextual symbols and personalized photos outperform PCS for initial identification (one RCT, TD toddlers). Photos > line drawings for people with ID (older data, [DIS]).

## 3. Thistle & Wilkinson: background color, clustering, consistent location

- **Wilkinson, Carlin & Thistle (2008)**, *AJSLP* 17:179–193. **N=26**: 8 TD <4 yrs, 8 TD >4 yrs, **10 children with Down syndrome [DIS]**; find target in 12-symbol array. **Clustering same-(internal)-color symbols sped search for ALL groups and improved accuracy for younger TD children and the DS group.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18448605/
- **Thistle & Wilkinson (2009)**, *AJSLP* 18. **[TD]** preschoolers: **background color cues did NOT speed locating targets** (line drawings); internal symbol color mattered instead. https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/1058-0360(2009/08-0029)
- **Wilkinson & Snell (2011)**, *AJSLP* 20:288–301. **N=30 [TD]** children, 8-choice emotion-symbol arrays: **spatial clustering by valence sped search — but only with WHITE backgrounds; adding background color removed the clustering benefit and did not itself help.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21813821/
- **Wilkinson & McIlvane (2013)**, *AJIDD* 118:353–364. **N=24 [DIS]** (12 Down syndrome, 12 ASD): responses significantly **faster when symbols clustered by internal color** vs. no arrangement cue. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24245729/
- **Thistle & Wilkinson (2017)**, *AAC* 33(3):160–169. **N=52 [TD]** young children, 4×4 (16-symbol) arrays, constructing multi-symbol messages; 2×2 background-color × word-class-arrangement. **Significantly faster when symbols were arranged (grouped/columned) by word class; background color gave no significant benefit — majority of children were actually faster with white backgrounds.** Authors: "caution when incorporating background color on displays for young children." https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28617614/ (erratum: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28670934/)
- **Thistle, Holmes, Horn & Reum (2018)**, *AJSLP* 27:1010–1017. **N=24 [TD]** preschoolers, 5 sessions, 9 trials/session: consistent-location group (n=12) vs. symbols relocated every session (n=12). **Session 1: no difference (≈6.0 s). Session 5: consistent group significantly faster (≈3.3 s).** Authors: consistent symbol location supports motor learning; **replication with children who use AAC is critical (not yet done).** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29860450/
- **Wilkinson, Gilmore & Qian (2022)**, *JSLHR* 65:710–726. **N=10 [DIS]** adolescents/adults with Down syndrome, eye tracking: **reducing visual crowding (spacing) + spatial grouping by grammatical category cut fixations to distracters; background color DID reduce latency in these older participants.** Age/experience may moderate the background-color effect. https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2021_JSLHR-21-00278
- Context: for **adults without disabilities in large 64-symbol arrays**, background color may help (Thistle & Wilkinson 2019, *Persp ASHA SIG*): https://pubs.asha.org/doi/abs/10.1044/2019_PERSP-19-00017

**Verification of the team's shorthand "cluster by color, plain backgrounds, fixed positions": VERIFIED**, with precision: cluster by the symbols' *internal* color / word class (STRONG, includes DS+ASD samples); plain/white backgrounds for young children in ≤16–24 symbol arrays (MODERATE-STRONG; may reverse for older users/large arrays); fixed positions (MODERATE — one study, TD only).

## 4. Grid size / number of symbols per screen

- **What has actually been tested with children:** Drager 2003/2004: dynamic-display systems, 12 target vocabulary items across pages **[TD 2.5–3 yrs]**. Wilkinson 2008: 12-symbol arrays **[TD + DS]**. Wilkinson & Snell 2011: 8-choice **[TD]**. Thistle & Wilkinson 2017 and Thistle 2018: **4×4 = 16-cell arrays [TD]**. Wilkinson, O'Neill & McIlvane 2014: eye tracking on clustered vs. distributed displays, school-age **[TD]**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24129007/
- **Team's belief check:** *"nothing past 16 cells was tested and only on TD preschoolers"* — **mostly correct but slightly overstated.** (a) Up to 16-symbol experimental arrays dominate the child literature; some background-color work references arrays up to 24; **64-symbol arrays were tested only with adults without disabilities**. (b) Not only TD preschoolers: the 12–16 symbol search studies included **children with Down syndrome (Wilkinson 2008; Wilkinson & McIlvane 2013) and ASD** — but these are visual-search tasks, not communication outcomes. **No study has parametrically varied grid size (e.g., 4 vs 16 vs 40 vs 60) with children who use AAC.** Light, Wilkinson et al. 2019 review confirms this gap. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6436972/
- **Petroi, Koul & Corwin (2014)** [DIS adults, Broca's aphasia]: more accurate/faster with **fewer navigation levels and smaller (4-symbol) arrays** — the closest thing to a size manipulation, and it's adults. (Cited in Light et al. 2019 review, URL above.)
- Related: **Gevarter et al. (2020)** *AAC* — display organization effects on symbol comprehension/use in **3 children with developmental delays [DIS]**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7253965/

## 5. Motor planning / LAMP

- **Bedwani, Bruck & Costley (2015)**, *Cogent Education*, "AAC for children with ASD: an evidence-based evaluation of the LAMP programme." **N=8 [DIS]** children with ASD and limited communication; pre/post (5 weeks + 2 weeks), **no control group**: all 8 gained spontaneous device communication and vocabulary; 100% commenting, 75–87% other social functions. This is the main peer-reviewed LAMP-branded outcome study — **uncontrolled, small, weak design.** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2331186X.2015.1045807 ; https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1087974
- **No peer-reviewed experimental comparison of a motor-planning (LAMP/Unity-style) layout vs. any alternative layout exists for children.** Vendor-compiled research lists exist (https://aacandautism.com/assets/uploads/Research-Supporting-LAMP4.pdf) but are mostly conference posters/case series.
- **Indirect mechanistic support:** Thistle et al. 2018 consistent-location motor learning (above, TD preschoolers); **Dukhovny & Gahl (2014)**, *J Commun Disord* 48:52–60 — in TD adults, motor-plan similarity on an SGD affected lexical recall, evidence that device-specific *motor* memory contributes to word retrieval. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24630143/
- **Verdict:** the *principle* (keep motor patterns consistent) has converging basic-science support; the *package/brand claim* is WEAK.

## 6. Navigation depth / dynamic display navigation

- **Drager et al. (2004)** (above): 3-year-olds struggled with all menu approaches initially; **VSD-thumbnail menus were navigated more accurately than symbol-based menu pages**; performance improved with learning sessions.
- **Quach & Beukelman (2010)**, *AAC* 26:1–11. **N=21 [TD]** 6–7-year-olds reproducing sentences on a dynamic-display device (dual-screen prototype); 7-year-olds more accurate/faster than 6-year-olds; instruction type mattered only for 6-year-olds; all generalized and maintained. Shows navigation of dynamic displays is learnable but developmentally graded even at 6–7. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20196699/
- **Petroi, Koul & Corwin (2014)** [DIS adults]: fewer levels = better (above).
- **O'Neill, Wilkinson & Light (2019)**, *AAC* 35:240–250. **N=55** (ASD n=13, DS n=13, other IDD n=9 [DIS]; TD preschoolers n=20): complex displays = main VSD + navigation bar of thumbnail VSDs in 4 positions. Both elements attracted attention; most time on meaningful elements (people/shared activity, ~76–81%); **navigation-bar location changed gaze patterns to the main scene — bar placement is a real design variable.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31305160/
- **No study establishes "how many taps deep" young children can go.** UNTESTED beyond the qualitative finding that every added level costs young children accuracy.

## 7. Eye-tracking: how children with ASD/DS scan displays

- **Wilkinson, O'Neill & McIlvane (2014)**, *JSLHR* 57:455–466. School-age **[TD]**: distributed (non-clustered) color layouts → slower first fixation on target and more fixations on irrelevant distracters. Clustering works by suppressing distracter fixations. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24129007/
- **Wilkinson & McIlvane (2013)** [DIS: DS n=12, ASD n=12]: same clustering benefit; visual search overall better in ASD than DS group. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24245729/
- **Wilkinson & Light (2014)** [DIS+TD, N=18]: humans in photos capture attention fast in all groups (above). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24773517/
- **O'Neill et al. (2019)** [DIS+TD, N=55]: navigation bar / main-VSD attention split (above).
- **Wilkinson, Gilmore & Qian (2022)** [DIS, DS n=10]: spacing to reduce crowding + category-based spatial grouping → fewer distracter fixations (above).
- **Wilkinson, Brittlebank, Barwise, Zimmerman & Light (2024)**, *AAC* 40:155–167. **N=19 [DIS]** (10 autistic, 9 DS): **visual fixation latency significantly correlated with motor selection latency** on VSD+nav-bar displays — validates eye tracking as proxy for selection efficiency, and confirms that what slows the eyes slows the hand. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12180136/ ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07434618.2024.2325065
- **Layout implications:** cluster related symbols spatially; keep internal-color groups together; leave white space (crowding hurts DS users); expect people in photos to dominate attention; nav-bar position matters.

## 8. Transition to Literacy (T2L)

Feature (Light, McNaughton, Jakobs & Hershberger 2014, RERC on AAC): selecting a symbol/hotspot triggers **dynamic animated text zooming to prominence + speech output**. Program page: https://rerc-aac.psu.edu/research/r2-investigating-aac-technologies-to-support-the-transition-from-graphic-symbols-to-literacy/

- **Caron, Light, Holyfield & McNaughton (2018)**, *AAC* 34:143–154. **N=5 [DIS]** individuals with ASD + CCN, multiple probe: T2L dynamic text **within a grid display** → all 5 increased accuracy on 12 targeted sight words with limited exposure. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29642715/
- **Mandak, Light & McNaughton (2019)**, *JADD* 49:1193–1204. **N=3 [DIS]** preliterate preschoolers (3–4) with ASD, shared digital book reading with dynamic text+speech: **all acquired target sight words with minimal exposure** (moderate–very large effects). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30430319/
- **Holyfield, Caron, Light & McNaughton (2019)**, *J Dev Phys Disabil* 31:727–740. **N=3 [DIS]** school-aged children with multiple disabilities; **video VSDs with hotspots + T2L** → single-word reading gains, Tau-U 0.69–0.84 (all p<.05). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10202467/
- **Caron, Light & McNaughton (2020)** *RPSD* (grid AAC app + T2L, individuals with CCN): https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1540796920911152 ; **(2021)** severe ASD replication: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1540796921992123
- **Cumulative summary** (Overcoming Barriers to Literacy review, 2025, PMC): **across nine T2L studies, 89% of 37 participants [DIS] showed single-word reading gains from exposure alone; mean Tau-U ≈ 0.80.** Intended to supplement, not replace, literacy instruction. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13122432/
- Adolescent CP replication (video VSD + dynamic text): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8297961/

Strong, replicated single-case evidence — directly supports a "grows with the child" board that surfaces text alongside symbols.

## 9. Just-in-time (JIT) programming

- **Caron, Light & Drager (2016)**, *AAC* 32:12–24. **N=8** professionals interacting with preschool children [TD]: app with fewer programming steps (EasyVSD) → **more VSDs+hotspots created live in 10-min interactions, higher child engagement** than GoTalk Now. Low operational demand is the design requirement for JIT. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26694519/
- **Drager, Light et al. (2019)** [DIS, N=9, ages 8–20, severe disability]: VSD+JIT → all increased symbolic turns (above). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31263379/
- **Holyfield, Caron, Drager & Light (2019)**, *AAC* — JIT programming of VSDs on mobile tech for **5 individuals with severe disability [DIS]** (cited within the above program of research; see also rerc summary https://rerc-aac.psu.edu/2018/06/01/1862-2/).
- Extension to adults (feasibility, 2025): https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07434618.2025.2462059
- **Design implication (verified):** capturing a photo in-the-moment and adding hotspots in seconds is an evidence-backed feature; effect shown on communication turns in disabled populations (single-case designs).

## 10. 2020–2026 design research: animation, AI symbols, touch access

**Animation:**
- **Jagaroo & Wilkinson (2008)**, *AAC* — theoretical case for animation leveraging motion perception (design framework, no data).
- **Mineo, Peischl & Pennington (2008)**, *AAC* 24:162–173. **[TD]** preschoolers, 4-choice arrays: **dynamic (animated) action representations identified better than static; most realistic dynamic best.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18465369/
- **Schlosser et al. (2014)**, *JSLHR* 57:1779–1791. **N≈220 [TD]** 3–5-year-olds: **animation improved NAMING of verb symbols** (ALP > PCS for prepositions); **animation did NOT improve identification** (receptive matching). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24811580/ (earlier: Schlosser et al. 2011 *JSLHR*: https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/1092-4388(2011/10-0164))
- **Schlosser, Brock, Koul, Shane & Flynn (2019)**, *JSLHR* 62:965–978. **[DIS]** children 3–7 with mild–severe ASD, randomized to animated vs. static ALP verb symbols: **animated symbols identified significantly better.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30986148/
- Net: animate VERBS (and consider for prepositions); no evidence animation helps nouns; keep animation on-demand to avoid distraction (crowding/attention literature).

**AI-generated / automated symbols & boards:**
- **Fontana de Vargas, Yu, Shane & Moffatt (2024)**, CHI '24, "Co-Designing QuickPic: Automated Topic-Specific Communication Boards from Photographs." 8 SLP participants; all willing to adopt; use case = JIT display creation. Feasibility/co-design, no child outcomes. https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642080 ; QuickPic AAC app paper: https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/21/9/1150
- **Exploring Generative AI to Support Automated JIT Programming for VSDs (2024, arXiv)** — stakeholder interviews; benefits (SLP effort reduction) vs. risks (personalization loss, culturally inappropriate outputs). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.11137
- No peer-reviewed study yet tests AI-generated symbols with child AAC users. UNTESTED for outcomes.

**Touch access for motor differences:**
- No strong 2020–2026 child-specific target-size/spacing literature for AAC; work is model-based (information-rate models for dyskinetic CP: https://doi.org/10.1109/TNSRE.2018.2840445) plus general accessibility standards (WCAG 2.5.8 minimum 24×24 px targets). Light, Wilkinson et al. 2019 flag alternative access (incl. eye gaze) as a critical untested area for the display-design principles. Demands of access methods for motor impairment: https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2022_AJSLP-22-00006

## 11. Layout that grows: organization schemes and scaling

- **Fallon, Light & Achenbach (2003)**, *AAC* 19:74–85. **N=20 [TD]** children 4;0–5;11 sorted 42 familiar vocabulary pictures + explained rationales. Purposeful arrangement of ~40% of items; **concrete items organized in pairs/small groups by EVENT SCHEMA (thematic), not taxonomic categories.** Adult taxonomic organization does not match preschool cognition; taxonomic organization emerges later. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/0743461031000112061 ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230852837
- **Drager et al. (2003)** grid-taxonomic vs. grid-schematic vs. scene-schematic: schematic scene best; taxonomic grids worst for 2.5-year-olds (above).
- **Light & Drager (2007)**, *AAC* 23:204–216, state of the science: learning demands of current systems too high for young children; systems must "grow" with the child. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17701740/ ; also Light & Drager (2002) *Assistive Technology* design requirements incl. "grow easily and seamlessly with children as they develop": https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12739847/
- **Transitioning few-large → many-small buttons without destroying motor patterns: NO direct study exists.** Relevant anchors: Thistle et al. 2018 (position consistency is what builds speed), Wilkinson et al. 2022 (crowding costs), Wilkinson et al. 2024 (visual→motor latency coupling), Dukhovny & Gahl 2014 (motor memory in recall), Petroi et al. 2014 (fewer levels/smaller arrays better, adults). Common clinical advice — start with the full target grid, mask/hide cells, reveal progressively so positions never move — is **logically consistent with these data but has never been experimentally tested against re-layouts.** Light, Wilkinson et al. 2019: "at what point the visual-perceptual cues begin to matter less… if a goal is to add vocabulary to a display that makes use of spatial cues" is an open question. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6436972/

## Design rules the evidence supports (with confidence)

| # | Design rule | Confidence | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | For beginning communicators (~≤3 yrs language stage), start with personalized photo VSDs with hotspots; people in the photo; expect faster learning than grids | **STRONG** (learnability, communication turns; single-case designs + CEC EBP status; note lab overlap) | Drager 03/04 [TD]; Patenaude 24 synthesis [DIS]; Gevarter 14 [DIS] |
| 2 | Include familiar people in VSD photos; humans capture attention within ~1.5 s across ASD/DS/IDD/TD | **STRONG** (attention) / MODERATE (communication benefit) | Wilkinson & Light 14; O'Neill 19 |
| 3 | Cluster symbols by internal color / word class; spatial grouping is the cue that works | **STRONG** (TD + DS + ASD, speed & accuracy, eye-tracking mechanism) | Wilkinson 08, 13, 14; Thistle & Wilkinson 17; Wilkinson 22 |
| 4 | Plain/white symbol backgrounds for young children in ≤16–24 cell arrays; do NOT rely on background color coding | **MODERATE-STRONG** [TD + some DS]; may reverse for older users / 64+ arrays | Thistle & Wilkinson 09, 17; Wilkinson & Snell 11; Wilkinson 22 |
| 5 | Keep symbol positions fixed; speed advantage emerges by ~5 sessions | **MODERATE** (one study, N=24, TD only; converging motor-memory data) | Thistle 18; Dukhovny & Gahl 14 |
| 6 | Leave spacing/white space; visual crowding costs search efficiency (esp. Down syndrome) | **MODERATE** | Wilkinson 22 |
| 7 | Organize early vocabulary schematically (by activity/event), not taxonomically | **MODERATE** (TD only, 2 studies) | Fallon 03; Drager 03 |
| 8 | Use VSD-thumbnail menus for navigation; minimize navigation depth | **MODERATE** (thumbnails: 1 TD study; depth: adult aphasia + review consensus). Exact tolerable depth: **UNTESTED** | Drager 04; Petroi 14; O'Neill 19 |
| 9 | Navigation bar placement changes what children look at — treat as a first-class design variable | **WEAK-MODERATE** (preliminary eye tracking) | O'Neill 19 |
| 10 | T2L: on symbol selection, show dynamic text animation + speech; teaches sight words through use | **STRONG** (9 single-case studies, 37 participants [DIS], 89% positive, mean Tau-U ≈ .80) | Caron 18/20/21; Mandak 19; Holyfield 19 |
