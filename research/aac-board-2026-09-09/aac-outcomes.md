# Evidence Base for AAC Interventions in Nonverbal/Minimally Verbal Children (Ages 3-12)
Research synthesis for AAC app product team. Compiled 2026-09-09. All sources peer-reviewed unless flagged.

---

## 1. RCTs / controlled trials of AAC and SGDs with minimally verbal children with autism

- **Kasari, Kaiser, Goods, Nietfeld, Mathy, Landa, Murphy & Almirall (2014).** *J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry*, 53(6), 635-646. **N=61**, minimally verbal children with autism, ages 5-8. **Design:** Sequential Multiple Assignment Randomized Trial (SMART) — blended developmental/behavioral intervention (JASP+EMT: joint attention/symbolic play + Enhanced Milieu Teaching) with or without a speech-generating device (SGD), 6 months + 3-month follow-up. Stage 2 adapted (intensify sessions or add SGD) based on early response. **Finding:** Beginning treatment WITH the SGD produced significantly greater spontaneous communicative utterances, novel words, and comments than spoken-only intervention. For slow responders, *adding* the SGD was more effective than *intensifying* the spoken-only intervention. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24839882/ ; https://www.jaacap.org/article/S0890-8567(14)00163-4/abstract
- **Almirall, DiStefano, Chang, Shire, Kaiser, Lu, Nahum-Shani, Landa, Mathy & Kasari (2016).** Longitudinal effects of adaptive interventions with an SGD. *J Clin Child Adolesc Psychol*, 45(4), 442-456. Same **N=61** SMART cohort; compared three 24-week adaptive interventions (no SGD initially / SGD only for slow responders / SGD from the start with intensification for slow responders). **Finding:** The adaptive intervention beginning with JASP+EMT+SGD was superior for total spontaneous communicative utterances (TSCU) and initiating joint attention (p<.05). At week 12 the SGD children produced ~19 more spontaneous communicative utterances than non-SGD peers; ~22 more at week 24. **CRITICAL CAVEAT — fading:** During the no-treatment follow-up (weeks 24-36) the SGD advantage shrank from ~22 to ~7 utterances. Gains erode substantially when intervention stops; effects are not self-sustaining. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26954267/ ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15374416.2016.1138407
- **DiStefano, Shih, Kaiser, Landa & Kasari (2016).** *Autism Research*, 9, 1093-1102. Secondary analysis of the same trial. Examined communication "interchanges" (back-and-forth exchanges); growth in interactional engagement accompanied SGD use. URL: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aur.1594
- **Kasari et al. (2025), AIM-ASD community SMART.** *J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry*. **N=194**, minimally verbal (<20 functional words) children with ASD ages 5-8, 4 US sites, delivered in community schools. SMART comparing DTT vs JASP-EMT first-stage, with adaptive second stages (parent training or combined DTT+JASP-EMT). **No SGD arm.** **Finding:** No difference between starting with DTT vs JASP-EMT on spontaneous communicative utterances (p=.41, ES=-0.1); best adaptive sequence gained only ~7.7 utterances over 16 weeks. Gains modest overall; adaptive structure > fixed. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12587618/
- **Chenausky, Norton, Tager-Flusberg & Schlaug (2016).** Auditory-Motor Mapping Training (AMMT) vs speech repetition control. *PLoS ONE*. **N=23** minimally verbal children with autism received ≥25 AMMT sessions (7 matched to 7 controls). Intonation-based (sung) treatment increased syllable approximations (+19.4%), consonants correct (+13.8%). Small N, matched comparison not full RCT. Not AAC per se but the main non-AAC speech treatment trial in this population. URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0164930
- **Gap 2015-2026:** No new large RCT of SGD-based intervention in minimally verbal autism has replicated Kasari 2014 at scale. The 2025 AIM-ASD trial dropped the SGD arm. The Kasari 2014/Almirall 2016 SMART remains the single best controlled evidence for SGDs in this exact population.

## 2. Does AAC hinder or help speech development? (the #1 parent fear)

- **Millar, Light & Schlosser (2006).** *JSLHR*, 49(2), 248-264. Research review, literature 1975-2003: 23 studies, **67 individuals** with developmental disabilities. Only 6 studies (27 cases) had adequate experimental control ("best-evidence" analysis). **Finding:** In the best-evidence cases, NO participant showed a decrease in speech following AAC intervention; the large majority (~89% of the 27 cases) showed gains — but gains were modest (often just a handful of words). URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16671842/ ; https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/1092-4388(2006/021)
- **Schlosser & Wendt (2008).** *AJSLP*, 17(3), 212-230. Systematic review, autism-specific, 1975-2007: 11 studies (9 single-case, 27 participants; 2 group studies, 98 participants). **Finding:** "AAC interventions do not impede speech production"; most studies showed increases, but gains were "rather modest" — clinicians should set realistic expectations. URL: https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/1058-0360(2008/021) ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK76412/
- **Romski et al. (2010) RCT (see §3):** toddlers in augmented conditions were **2.9x and 4.8x more likely** to produce spoken target vocabulary than the spoken-only condition — experimental (RCT-level) evidence that AAC *helps* rather than hinders speech.
- **Walters, Sevcik & Romski (2021).** *AJSLP*. Secondary analysis of two RCTs (109 completers). AAC-group children who spoke produced 1-21 different spoken target words (M=5.58) vs 1-4 (M=1.44) in the spoken-only group; speech-sound accuracy did not differ (children ~86% phoneme-accurate) — AAC did not degrade articulation. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8702839/
- **Kasari 2014 (see §1):** the SGD arm produced MORE novel spoken words — again, device use increased speech.
- **Bottom line with numbers:** across 2 systematic reviews (~125 participants) and 3 RCT datasets (~170 children), zero credible evidence of AAC suppressing speech; consistent evidence of modest speech *gains*, with RCT evidence (Romski, Kasari) showing AAC conditions beat speech-only conditions on spoken outcomes. Quality: reviews rest heavily on single-case designs (weak); the RCTs are the strongest rebuttal.

## 3. Early AAC intervention with toddlers/preschoolers (Romski & Sevcik)

- **Romski, Sevcik, Adamson, Cheslock, Smith, Barker & Bakeman (2010).** *JSLHR*, 53, 350-364. **RCT, N=68 randomized / 62 completed**, toddlers (24-36 mo) with developmental delay and <10 spoken words, parent-coached. Three arms: augmented communication-input (AC-I), augmented communication-output (AC-O, child uses SGD), spoken communication (SC). **Finding:** Both augmented conditions produced larger total (augmented+spoken) vocabularies; children in AC-O and AC-I were 2.9x / 4.8x more likely to produce spoken target words than SC. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20360461/ ; https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/1092-4388(2009/08-0156)
- **Adamson, Romski, Bakeman & Sevcik (2010).** *JSLHR*, 53. Same trial: augmented intervention increased symbol-infused joint engagement. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20705741/
- **Barker, Akaba, Brady & Thiemann-Bourque (2013)** and related secondary analyses show parent input moderates gains (parent involvement §8).
- Population note: developmental delay broadly (not autism-specific); ages 2-3. Still the best early-AAC RCT evidence in existence.

## 4. Aided language modeling / aided language stimulation

- **Sennott, Light & McNaughton (2016).** AAC modeling intervention research review. *Research & Practice for Persons with Severe Disabilities*, 41(2), 101-115. Best-evidence review: 9 single-case studies (31 participants, 70 replications) + 1 quasi-experimental group study (N=63). **Finding:** partner modeling on the AAC system during natural interaction showed "positive and large effects" on pragmatics, semantics, syntax, morphology for beginning communicators. URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1540796916638822
- **O'Neill, Light & Pope (2018).** Meta-analysis. *JSLHR*, 61(7), 1743-1765. 26 single-case designs (**88 participants**) + 2 group designs (**103 participants**). **Effect size: mean Tau-U = 0.83** (range -0.18 to 1.0) — very large; effective for both comprehension and expression. URL: https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2018_JSLHR-L-17-0132
- **Biggs, Carter & Gilson (2018).** Systematic review. *AJIDD*, 123(5), 443-473. **48 studies, 267 participants** (birth-21). Three modeling approaches (augmented input, models-as-prompts, models within instruction) all generally effective on expressive communication. **Caveat:** generalization evidence mixed in 40% of studies and absent in 26%. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30198767/ ; https://doi.org/10.1352/1944-7558-123.5.443
- **Quality flag:** almost entirely single-case experimental designs; Tau-U inflates relative to group-design effect sizes. Still, this is the most consistent, replicated finding in child AAC research: adults modeling on the device works. Design implication: an app should make partner modeling first-class.

## 5. Cochrane and other systematic reviews

- **Brignell, Chenausky, Song, Zhu, Suo & Morgan (2018).** Cochrane review: Communication interventions for ASD in minimally verbal children. **Only 2 RCTs, N=154** (ages 32 mo-11 yr): Focused Playtime Intervention (Siller 2013, N=70) and teacher-delivered PECS (Howlin 2007, N=84). **GRADE: VERY LOW certainty on all outcomes** (risk of bias, imprecision, one trial per comparison). PECS produced immediate gains in verbal initiations and symbol use, but **gains were NOT maintained 10 months later**; FPI: no significant standardized expressive-language gains. Conclusion: "limited evidence" overall. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30395694/ ; https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012324.pub2 ; summary: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6516977/
- **Ganz, Earles-Vollrath, Heath, Parker, Rispoli & Duran (2012).** Meta-analysis of single-case aided-AAC studies in ASD. *JADD*, 42, 60-74. 24 studies. **IRD = 0.99** for communication outcomes (very large); PECS and SGDs equally effective (IRD 0.99) vs other picture systems (0.61). Single-case metric — not comparable to Cohen's d. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21380612/
- **Leonet, Orcasitas-Vicandi, Langarika-Rocafort, Idoiaga Mondragon & Roman Etxebarrieta (2022).** Systematic review of AAC for children 0-6. *LSHSS*, 53(3), 894-920. 29/1,709 studies included; improvements across expressive/receptive/functional communication with SGDs and PECS most studied; mostly small-N designs. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35759607/
- **Note the contrast:** single-case meta-analyses look spectacular (IRD/Tau-U ≈ .8-.99) while the Cochrane view of RCT evidence is "very low certainty." Both are true: AAC reliably works in tightly controlled teaching contexts; large-trial, generalized, durable outcomes are thinly documented.

## 6. Core vs fringe vocabulary — evidence vs convention

- **Banajee, DiCarlo & Buras Stricklin (2003).** *AAC*, 19(2), 67-73. **N=50 TYPICALLY DEVELOPING toddlers** (24-36 mo), language sampled in play + snack. Nine words used by all 50 children across both routines; a ~23-26-word list covered 96.3% of tokens. **This is a descriptive frequency study of speaking TD toddlers, not an intervention study of AAC users.** URL: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/0743461031000112034
- **Trembath, Balandin & Togher (2007).** *J Intellect Dev Disabil*, 32(4), 291-301. **N=6 TD Australian preschoolers.** Small core + large individualized fringe vocabularies. Same limitation. URL: https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ780350
- **van Tilborg & Deckers (2016).** *Perspectives ASHA SIG 12*, 1(12), 125-138. Review concluding core vocabulary is comparable across typical and atypical populations/contexts — but based on language-sample studies, not outcomes. URL: https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/persp1.SIG12.125
- **Laubscher & Light (2020).** Narrative review. *AAC*, 36(1), 43-53. **Critique:** word categories dominating early TD vocabulary development (names, nouns, personally relevant words) are under-represented on core lists; frequency-based core words may be developmentally inappropriate for children at the earliest stages of symbolic communication. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32172598/
- **Status of evidence:** there is essentially **NO experimental trial showing core-word-first layouts produce better communication outcomes than interest-based/fringe-rich vocabularies** for beginning communicators. Core-vocabulary dogma is convention built on descriptive TD data. A first head-to-head vocabulary-intervention comparison only appeared in 2025 (*LSHSS*, https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2025_LSHSS-24-00059). Product implication: don't hard-code core-word orthodoxy; support personalized, motivating vocabulary alongside core.

## 7. Device abandonment / discontinuation

- **Johnson, Inglebret, Jones & Ray (2006).** *AAC*, 22(2), 85-99. Survey of **275 ASHA SIG-12 SLPs** (+6 focus groups). SLPs estimated that only **~39% of their clients continued using AAC for more than one year** after introduction (i.e., ~60% effective discontinuation within a year). Success factors: support, attitude, system fit; abandonment factors: lack of maintenance/adjustment, poor attitude, lack of training, lack of support, poor fit. **Caveat: clinician-report, not device tracking; 2006-era devices.** URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17114167/ ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07434610500483588
- **Moorcroft, Scarinci & Meyer (2019a).** Systematic review of barriers/facilitators to low-tech and unaided AAC. *Disabil Rehabil Assist Technol*, 14(7), 710-731. Key barriers: partner skill/training gaps, family support, system-family mismatch. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30070927/
- **Moorcroft, Scarinci & Meyer (2019b).** SLP perspectives on acceptance vs rejection/abandonment of AAC for children. *AAC*, 35(3), 193-204. Family factors (buy-in, training, support) dominate reasons children's systems are rejected or shelved.
- General assistive-technology literature (Phillips & Zhao 1993, older) puts AT abandonment near ~1/3; secondary sources claim "up to 75% never used successfully" — **treat these headline numbers as WEAK: old, mixed populations, self-report.** There is **no modern, large-scale, prospective usage-log study of child SGD abandonment** — a genuine gap (and an opportunity: an app can measure it directly). Recent conceptual reframing: Donato et al. 2023, capability-approach model of abandonment, https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07434618.2023.2199859

## 8. Predictors of AAC/communication success

- **Joint attention & play:** In Kasari-network work, baseline joint attention and symbolic play predicted spoken-language improvement in minimally verbal children (e.g., concurrent predictors study, *JADD* 2019, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-019-04089-8 ; DiStefano 2016 above).
- **Imitation:** Chenausky, Norton, Tager-Flusberg & Schlaug (2018), *Autism Research* — pre-treatment **phoneme imitation ability** (not age, not baseline severity) predicted speech improvement in minimally verbal children (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aur.2006).
- **Timing/dosage of SGD:** Kasari 2014/Almirall 2016 — starting with the SGD beat adding it later; slow responders given the SGD early spoke more by endpoint than those who got it at week 12. Adding the SGD beat adding *more hours* of spoken-only therapy — modality mattered more than dose.
- **Parent involvement:** Romski 2010 was fully parent-implemented (parents learned in 24 sessions); Biggs et al. 2022, *JADD*, "Building Family Capacity" showed multiple family members can learn aided modeling (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-022-05492-4). Kasari 2025 found adding parent training for early responders was part of the best adaptive sequence.
- **Quality flag:** these are moderator/correlational analyses within trials or small samples — MODERATE at best; no validated predictive model exists for "who will succeed with AAC."

## 9. Word prediction for CHILDREN using AAC

- **Almost all rate-enhancement/prediction research is with literate adults** (ALS, cerebral palsy) or typing simulations. Trnka, McCaw, Yarrington, McCoy & Pennington (2008-2009): higher-accuracy prediction improved simulated AAC communication rate by up to **58.6%**, and utilization rose with accuracy (93.6% vs 78.2%) — adult/keystroke-based (https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/1497302.1497307 ; https://www.eecis.udel.edu/~mccoy/publications/2008/trnka08at.pdf).
- **Children with physical disabilities:** Tam, Reid, et al. and Mezei & Heller-type classroom studies show word prediction can raise typing speed/spelling accuracy for some children (e.g., 7/10 fastest with prediction), but effects are individual and cognitive-load costs of scanning prediction lists are real (ERIC: https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1149999.pdf).
- **For 3-12-year-old symbol-based, preliterate AAC users: evidence is essentially ABSENT.** Nothing shows next-word prediction helps (or harms) young symbol communicators; the nearest analog is emerging "context-aware" AI display work (Griffen, Raley, Holyfield, MacNeil, Lorah & Burns, 2026, *J Special Ed Technology* — context-generated response options increased response frequency/accuracy/speed for autistic children in academic contexts; single-case, tiny N: https://doi.org/10.1177/01626434261420393). Treat any prediction feature claim for young children as unvalidated.

## 10. Sign vs SGD vs PECS comparisons and preference

- **Lorah, Parnell, Whitby & Hantula (2015).** Systematic review of tablet/iPod SGDs in ASD. *JADD*, 45, 3792-3804 (PMID 25413144). **17 studies, 57 participants ages 3-23.** 93% acquired SGD requesting; acquisition often faster with tablet SGD than PECS or manual sign; **16 of 19 participants assessed showed clear preference for the SGD** over PECS/sign. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25413144/
- **van der Meer, Rispoli, Sigafoos et al. (2012a/b).** *Res Dev Disabil* single-case comparisons (N=4 each): most children learned SGD fastest; 3/4 preferred SGD; acquisition/maintenance better for the *preferred* modality. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22554812/
- **Yong, Dutt, Chen & Yeong (2021).** *Child Lang Teach Ther* — preschoolers, PECS vs iPad SGD requesting: comparable acquisition, individual preferences again mostly device. URL: https://doi.org/10.1177/0265659021989391
- **Caveat:** virtually all comparison studies teach **requesting only**; a 2026 systematic review notes the field's predominant focus is still teaching requests (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42059383/). Preference data are small-N but remarkably consistent toward devices.

## 11. Telepractice / parent-coached AAC (2020-2026)

- **Dodge-Chin, Shigetomi-Toyama & Quinn (2022).** *LSHSS*, 53(2), 237-255. Telepractice parent coaching of RAAP (Read, Ask, Answer, Prompt) during book reading. **N=5** children (3;4-12;9) using AAC, randomized multiple-probe design: parents learned strategies via telehealth; child communication turns increased. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35050723/
- **Bekteshi et al. (2023).** Teleintervention for AAC users: systematic review. *Dev Med Child Neurol*. Telehealth delivery feasible and generally comparable to in-person for training partners; evidence base small and heterogeneous. URL: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/dmcn.15387
- Parent-perception studies of group tele-AAC (e.g., PMC9620687) report acceptability. **Overall: feasibility-level (WEAK-MODERATE) evidence; no adequately powered tele-AAC RCT for this population yet.** COVID-era practice outran the science.

## 12. Genuinely new, 2023-2026

- **Binger et al. (2026).** "AAC Generative Language Intervention: A Randomized Controlled Trial." *JSLHR*, 69(6), 2654-2678. **N=40 preschoolers (3;0-4;11)** with severe speech impairment and intact receptive language (CP, apraxia, etc. — **not autism**). AAC-GLI (28 sessions, SGDs + activity displays) vs workshop-only control. Intervention group grew ~0.26 symbols/month in weighted MLU vs ~0.10 (ns) in controls; **Hedges' g = 0.56-0.77** at all post-baseline points, maintained. First modern well-powered RCT showing AAC intervention builds *grammar*, not just requests. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13288823/
- **Kasari et al. (2025) AIM-ASD** (§1): N=194 community SMART — largest-ever trial in minimally verbal ASD; sobering modest gains.
- **NIH/NIDCD:** Jan 2023 NIDCD workshop on interventions for minimally verbal/non-speaking autistic people (chairs: Cooper, Kasari, Tager-Flusberg) — https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/news/events/minimally-verbalnon-speaking-individuals-autism-research-directions-interventions ; first targeted NIH funding call for this population (https://www.thetransmitter.org/spectrum/a-catalyst-for-change-nih-makes-first-call-for-research-supporting-minimally-verbal-autistic-people/). Tager-Flusberg's BU ACE (CARE) continues to publish on profiles/heterogeneity of minimally verbal children (e.g., "Profiles of minimally verbal autistic children," *Autism* 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38803132/).
- **AI in AAC:** Griffen et al. 2026 context-aware AAC with automated response options (§9); Holyfield-line AI emotion-expression AAC pilot with autistic and non-autistic children, *AJSLP* 2026 (https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2026_AJSLP-25-00469); "Future of AAC technologies: priorities for inclusive innovation," *AAC* 2025 (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07434618.2025.2513906); foundation-models-in-AAC opportunities/challenges preprint (arXiv, **not peer-reviewed**: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.08866). **All AI-AAC child evidence is pilot-level; nothing outcome-validated at trial scale.**

## What the evidence actually supports

**STRONG (multiple converging designs incl. RCTs):**
- AAC does not hinder speech; it modestly helps. (Millar 2006; Schlosser & Wendt 2008; Romski 2010 RCT — 2.9-4.8x more likely to speak target words; Walters 2021; Kasari 2014.)
- Adding an SGD to naturalistic developmental-behavioral intervention improves spontaneous communication in minimally verbal 5-8-year-olds with autism, and starting with the device early beats adding it later or adding therapy hours. (Kasari 2014; Almirall 2016.)
- Aided language modeling by partners improves expressive/receptive outcomes (Tau-U ≈ 0.83 across ~190 participants in 3 syntheses) — strongest within single-case methodology; group-design confirmation thinner.
- Most children can rapidly acquire basic SGD requesting (93% across Lorah 2015) and most prefer devices over PECS/sign (16/19).

**MODERATE:**
- Early (toddler/preschool) augmented intervention improves vocabulary and grammar (Romski 2010; Binger 2026 RCT g=0.56-0.77 — but Binger's sample is speech-impaired, not autistic/ID).
- Joint attention, play, and imitation as predictors of response; parent-implemented delivery works when parents are coached.
- Telepractice partner training is feasible and promising (small-N designs only).

**WEAK:**
- Durability: gains fade when intervention stops (Almirall 2016 advantage shrank 22→7 utterances; Howlin PECS gains gone at 10 months). Cochrane rates the whole RCT base "very low certainty."
- Abandonment figures (~60% within a year, "one-third of AT abandoned") — old, clinician-reported, not device-logged.
- Core-vocabulary superiority: descriptive TD data only (Banajee N=50 TD toddlers; Trembath N=6 TD), actively critiqued (Laubscher & Light 2020); no outcome trials.

**ABSENT:**
- Word prediction benefits for young/symbol-based AAC users; any validated AI-AAC outcomes in children; a modern prospective abandonment/usage study of child SGDs; adequately powered tele-AAC RCTs; any RCT comparing vocabulary organizations or layouts in this population.
