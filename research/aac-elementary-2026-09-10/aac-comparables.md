# AAC app comparables: consumer sales evidence

Prepared for Frank Schaeffer, EZ Voice LLC (EZvoxa, planned $99.99 one-time).
All figures read on 10 September 2026 unless stated. Sources are limited to the app stores (Apple lookup API and listing pages, Google Play listing pages), the makers' own sites, and Dynavox Group's own annual report. No analytics sites, forums, news, or Wikipedia were used.

Method notes:
- Apple figures come from `https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=<id>&country=us` (fields: `price`, `userRatingCount`, `averageUserRating`, `primaryGenreName`, `contentAdvisoryRating`, `releaseDate`). In-app purchase prices come from the US listing page at `https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id<id>`.
- Google Play figures come from `https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=<package>&hl=en_US&gl=US`: the "Downloads" range as shown, the JSON-LD `ratingCount` and `ratingValue`, `contentRating`, and the "In-app purchases" price range as shown.
- Neither store publishes unit sales, revenue, or buyer ages. "Not published" below means exactly that.
- Apple category rank: the lookup API has no rank field, and none of the US listing pages fetched contained a "#N in <category>" line. Not published for any app checked.
- Google Play "Released on" date: the fetched HTML did not contain it for any listing; only "Updated on" was present. Recorded as not published here.

## What this supports

1. The stores publish rating counts and install ranges, not sales, so nothing here is a unit-sales figure, and I have not converted ratings or installs into sales.
2. Proloquo2Go's 12,081 US ratings at $249.99 stand at least 25 times above any other paid symbol-based AAC app on the App Store (Speak for Yourself 404, TouchChat WordPower 131, LAMP 93, Avaz Lifetime 74, GoTalk NOW 2), which is consistent with AssistiveWare's own "over 500,000 AAC apps downloaded" across its apps and "hundreds of thousands" for Proloquo2Go, but those maker figures mix free and paid apps and are not a paid-unit count.
3. The one-time price band for full symbol AAC apps is $119.99 to $299.99 (GoTalk NOW, TouchChat HD, Proloquo2Go, TouchChat WordPower, LAMP, Speak for Yourself, Avaz Lifetime), and lifetime tiers inside free apps sit at $124.99 to $299.99 (Spoken, Leeloo $149, Sayella $199.99, Avaz $199.99/$299.99, Grid $299.99, CoughDrop $294.99), so $99.99 would sit below every established symbol-based competitor and level with PRC's Dialogue AAC.
4. On Google Play the largest AAC install range belongs to a $10.99-IAP text-to-speech app (Speech Assistant AAC, 500K+), followed by free-with-subscription symbol apps at 100K+ (Avaz, Spoken, Leeloo, Cboard, Talkie, Expressia, SymboTalk); no paid-up-front symbol AAC app on Play shows more than 5K+ (TalkTablet PRO), which suggests Android AAC volume is concentrated in free or low-priced apps.
5. The major device makers (PRC-Saltillo, Tobii Dynavox) either do not ship on Android at all or ship only companion apps there, and AssistiveWare is iOS-only by its own account.
6. The only audited revenue in the industry is Dynavox Group's: SEK 1,972.1 million in 2024, 78% North America, 69% gross margin, roughly 90% paid by insurers and public funders, with no unit count published; it measures the prescribed-device market, not consumer app sales, and cannot be scaled to app units.
7. Dynavox Group's own market estimate, "50 million people globally in need", "around 2 million diagnosed annually", "only around 2% get access", is a company estimate in an annual report, not a government figure, and should be labeled as such if reused.
8. Nothing in any store or maker page separates teen or young-adult buyers; the only apps that name teens in their own positioning are Spoken ("teens and adults"), Sayella ("literate teens and adults"), Proloquo4Text ("adults, teens and children"), and PRC-Saltillo's site ("children, teens and adults").
9. Rating counts on the App Store compound since first release (Proloquo2Go since 2009, LAMP since 2012), so they compare lifetime traction, not current-year volume, and newer apps (Sayella, July 2026, 0 ratings) are not yet measurable.
10. To turn any of this into a sales estimate would require a ratings-to-sales or installs-to-sales ratio that no source here publishes; that ratio would be an assumption and must be labeled as one if Frank chooses to use it.
